Why Physical Activity Matters

Why Staying Active and Keeping Muscle Matters

Physical activity matters well beyond appearance. A global analysis estimated that 7.2% of all-cause deaths worldwide, about one in every 14, could be attributed to physical inactivity, which makes regular movement a cornerstone of individual health.

Building and keeping muscle mass is a key part of this. Muscle is not just for strength or movement: it is the body’s largest store of protein and amino acids. During serious illness, the body mobilises amino acids from skeletal muscle to produce the proteins and glucose it needs to respond, so people who enter a health crisis with more muscle have more to draw on. A meta-analysis found that sarcopenia, the loss of muscle mass and function, increases the risk of death among critically ill patients. Muscle wasting can start early in a hospital stay, at roughly 2% of muscle per day in the first week, which is why a larger muscle “reserve” built through regular activity, especially resistance training, can make recovery easier.

How Much Activity Do We Need?

Adults

Adults between 18 and 64 should aim for 150 to 300 minutes of moderate physical activity per week. If the activity is vigorous, 75 to 150 minutes is enough.

Children

Children and adolescents should average about 60 minutes of activity per day.
